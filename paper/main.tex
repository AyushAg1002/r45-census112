\begin{abstract}
Angeltveit and McKay determined the $30{,}976$ Ramsey $(4,5,22)$ graphs
with $113$ edges.  We use their complete catalogue to determine the entire
non-$K_4$-saturated part of the next layer.  Up to isomorphism, exactly
$785{,}888$ Ramsey $(4,5,22)$ graphs with $112$ edges are not
$K_4$-saturated.  Two separately implemented deletion-and-filter programs
emit the same $887{,}138$ valid deletion records byte-for-byte.
The nauty and Traces canonical-labeling algorithms give the same class count,
and nauty recanonicalization makes the catalogues byte-identical.  A
standard-library checker validates every representative.  We also prove
computationally that every Ramsey $(4,5,22)$ graph with $112$ edges has
minimum degree at least $7$, by exhausting one-vertex extensions from the
complete $106$- and $107$-edge catalogues at order $21$.  A directly audited
$K_4$-saturated example shows that the complete layer has at least
$785{,}889$ classes; its saturated residue remains uncensused.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Ramsey graph; graph census; saturated graph;
canonical labeling; computer-assisted proof.\\
\textbf{MSC 2020:} 05C55 (primary); 05C30, 05C35 (secondary).

\section{Introduction}

For positive integers $s,t,n,e$, let $\Rcal(s,t,n,e)$ denote the set of
isomorphism classes of graphs on $n$ vertices and $e$ edges that contain
neither a clique of order $s$ nor an independent set of order $t$.  We omit
$e$ when no edge count is prescribed.  The classical equality
$R(4,5)=25$ means that $\Rcal(4,5,24)$ is nonempty but
$\Rcal(4,5,25)$ is empty \cite{mckay-radziszowski-1995}.

High-edge portions of $\Rcal(4,5,n)$ are important local inputs in current
work on the diagonal Ramsey number $R(5,5)$.  Angeltveit and McKay recently
proved $R(5,5)\leq 46$ \cite{angeltveit-mckay-2026}.  As part of that work
they completed the high-edge catalogues at orders $21$, $22$, and $23$.
In particular,
\[
 |\Rcal(4,5,22,114)|=133,
 \qquad
 |\Rcal(4,5,22,113)|=30{,}976.
\]
Their published table estimates that the unrestricted order-$22$ class has
roughly $1.8\times 10^{15}$ members, making an unstructured census of the
next edge layer unattractive.

This paper isolates a sector of the $112$-edge layer that is exactly
recoverable from the existing $113$-edge catalogue.  A $K_4$-free graph is
\emph{$K_4$-saturated} when adding any nonedge creates a $K_4$.  Define
\[
 \Xcal_{112}
 =\{G\in\Rcal(4,5,22,112):G\text{ is not }K_4\text{-saturated}\}.
\]
Our main computational result is
\[
 \boxed{|\Xcal_{112}|=785{,}888.}
\]
The qualifier ``not $K_4$-saturated'' is essential: we also construct and
audit a saturated graph in the same edge layer.

\begin{samepage}
The contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item We prove an elementary edge-deletion correspondence and use it to
  exhaust $\Xcal_{112}$ from the complete $113$-edge input catalogue.
  \item We reproduce the exhaustive deletion and Ramsey filtering with a
  separate implementation, cross-check canonical labeling with nauty and
  Traces, validate all $785{,}888$ representatives with a standard-library
  checker, and repeat the method one edge higher as a regression test.
  \item We exclude minimum degrees $5$ and $6$ in the full $112$-edge layer
  using two structurally different exhaustive extension programs.
  \item We give a deterministic SAT encoding and an audited witness proving
  that the remaining saturated sector is nonempty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{samepage}

The result does not improve the numerical upper bound on $R(5,5)$.  It is a
reproducible local-catalogue calculation that narrows one of the layers that
can arise in future approaches to that problem.

\section{The edge-deletion correspondence}

For a $K_4$-free graph $G$, call a nonedge $uv$ \emph{addable} if $G+uv$
is still $K_4$-free.  Let $a(G)$ denote the number of addable nonedges.  A
\emph{valid one-edge deletion} of a Ramsey $(4,5)$ graph is an edge deletion
whose result is still free of independent $5$-sets.

\begin{lemma}[Edge-deletion correspondence]\label{lem:correspondence}
A graph $G$ belongs to $\Xcal_{112}$ if and only if it is isomorphic to a
valid one-edge deletion of a graph in $\Rcal(4,5,22,113)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $G\in\Xcal_{112}$.  There is an addable nonedge $uv$.
Adding an edge cannot create an independent set, so $G+uv$ has neither a
$K_4$ nor an independent set of order $5$.  Thus
$G+uv\in\Rcal(4,5,22,113)$.

Conversely, let $H\in\Rcal(4,5,22,113)$ and suppose that $G=H-uv$ remains
free of independent sets of order $5$.  Edge deletion cannot create a
$K_4$, so $G\in\Rcal(4,5,22,112)$.  Restoring $uv$ gives the $K_4$-free
graph $H$, hence $uv$ is addable and $G$ is not $K_4$-saturated.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:correspondence} reduces mathematical coverage of the
nonsaturated sector to completeness of the $113$-edge input catalogue and
exhaustive processing of its edges.  It does not assume that a child has a
unique parent or a unique deleted edge; all duplication is removed after
generation.

\section{Exhaustive generation and verification}

\subsection{Input and primary pipeline}

The input is the complete public catalogue of $30{,}976$ nonisomorphic
members of $\Rcal(4,5,22,113)$ described in
\cite{angeltveit-mckay-2026,catalogue}.  Its graph6 file has hash
\begin{center}
\small\ttfamily
1e1a54b719dcdebb57581ee6f3cd4e1721e828680a72128010f1f888a4dda9db.
\end{center}
Every input has $113$ edges, so deleting each edge gives
$30{,}976\cdot113=3{,}500{,}288$ records.  A child automatically remains
$K_4$-free.  We retain it precisely when its independence number is at most
$4$, and then remove isomorphs.  Table~\ref{tab:pipeline} gives the exact
counts.

\begin{center}
\refstepcounter{table}Table~\thetable: Primary edge-deletion census.\label{tab:pipeline}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Stage & Records \\
\midrule
Published $113$-edge inputs & $30{,}976$ \\
All one-edge deletions & $3{,}500{,}288$ \\
Deletions remaining in $\Rcal(4,5)$ & $887{,}138$ \\
Nonisomorphic outputs & $785{,}888$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The primary implementation used \texttt{deledgeg}, \texttt{pickg}, and
\texttt{shortg} from nauty and Traces version 2.9300
\cite{mckay-1998,mckay-piperno-2014}.  In pseudocode:
\begin{quote}
\small
for each $H$ in the $113$-edge catalogue:\\
\hspace*{1em}for each $e\in E(H)$:\\
\hspace*{2em}if $\alpha(H-e)\leq4$, output $H-e$;\\
canonically label all outputs and retain one representative per class.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Cross-checks}

Five checks address different failure modes.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A standalone C++20 program uses its own graph6 parser and encoder,
  enumerates every source edge, and tests the independent-$5$ condition with
  bitsets.  If $uv\in E(H)$, deleting $uv$ can create only an independent
  $5$-set containing both $u$ and $v$; its other three vertices form an
  independent triple among their common nonneighbours.  The program tests
  this equivalent condition.  It emits exactly the same $887{,}138$ graph6
  records as the primary filter, byte-for-byte.  Canonicalizing them
  reproduces the primary $785{,}888$-record catalogue byte-for-byte.

  \item Ordinary nauty canonicalization gives $785{,}888$ classes.
  The distinct Traces algorithm gives the same count.  The two
  conventions produce different byte strings, as expected, but applying
  ordinary-nauty canonicalization to the Traces catalogue gives a file
  byte-for-byte identical to the primary catalogue.

  \item A separate Python program, using only the standard library, parses
  every final graph6 record and verifies order $22$, edge count $112$, no
  $K_4$, no independent $5$-set, and at least one addable nonedge.  This
  check does not call nauty or Traces.

  \item The final uncompressed catalogue has \sha{} hash
  \begin{center}
  \small\ttfamily
  d2c556f52d13dd4d38ed955bedd3db5f52b883faafc45fe5561e611afa5cd6a2.
  \end{center}
  The deterministic gzip archive has hash
  \begin{center}
  \small\ttfamily
  fcb0ef605eee3150d95d5b91535934ba9b711450a2edb7e55fd39a37ec3211d5.
  \end{center}

  \item As a one-level-up regression, deleting edges from all $133$ public
  members of $\Rcal(4,5,22,114)$ gives $15{,}162$ records, of which $4{,}077$
  remain Ramsey $(4,5)$ graphs.  Isomorphism rejection leaves $3{,}296$
  classes, and every one occurs in the published $30{,}976$-member
  $113$-edge catalogue.  The pinned $114$-edge input hash begins
  \texttt{54dffec4ecab0f86}; its full value is in the manifest.
\end{enumerate}

The last check yields an additional exact split.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:e113split}
Of the $30{,}976$ graphs in $\Rcal(4,5,22,113)$, exactly $3{,}296$ are not
$K_4$-saturated and exactly $27{,}680$ are $K_4$-saturated.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:correspondence} with edge counts $113$ and $114$, using
the complete published $113$- and $114$-edge catalogues, and subtract from
$30{,}976$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the pinned inputs and computational methods described above, and
conditional on completeness of the published catalogue
$\Rcal(4,5,22,113)$,
\[
 |\Xcal_{112}|=785{,}888.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:correspondence} proves coverage by valid edge deletion.
The two exhaustive deletion-and-filter computations produce $887{,}138$
valid deletion records.  Canonical labeling produces $785{,}888$
isomorphism classes.  The two canonical-labeling algorithms, bytewise
catalogue comparisons, and direct property audit described above agree.
\end{proof}

\section{Minimum degree in the full $112$-edge layer}

The degree conclusion suggested by Table~\ref{tab:degrees} extends to the
unknown saturated residue.

\begin{lemma}[One-vertex extension criterion]\label{lem:extension}
Let $H\in\Rcal(4,5,21)$ and let $S\subseteq V(H)$.  Add a new vertex $v$
with $N(v)=S$.  The resulting graph is in $\Rcal(4,5,22)$ if and only if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
  \item $H[S]$ is triangle-free, and
  \item $S$ meets every independent $4$-set of $H$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Any newly created $K_4$ must contain $v$, and its other three vertices form
a triangle in $H[S]$.  Any newly created independent $5$-set must contain
$v$, and its other four vertices form an independent set disjoint from $S$.
These are the only new forbidden subgraphs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mindegree}
Conditional on completeness of the published $106$- and $107$-edge
order-$21$ catalogues, every $G\in\Rcal(4,5,22,112)$ satisfies
\[
 7\leq\delta(G)\leq10.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be a minimum-degree vertex and put $d=d(v)$.  Then
$G-v\in\Rcal(4,5,21,112-d)$.  The published exact maximum edge count
$E(4,5,21)=107$ \cite{angeltveit-mckay-2026,catalogue} gives $d\geq5$,
while the average degree $224/22<11$ gives $d\leq10$.

For $d=5$, the seed $G-v$ must be one of the $31$ public members of
$\Rcal(4,5,21,107)$.  We tested all
$31\binom{21}{5}=630{,}819$ possible neighbourhoods using
Lemma~\ref{lem:extension}; none meets every independent $4$-set.

For $d=6$, the seed must be one of the $10{,}188$ public members of
$\Rcal(4,5,21,106)$.  Direct subset enumeration tests
$10{,}188\binom{21}{6}=552{,}841{,}632$ neighbourhoods.  Exactly $443$
meet every independent $4$-set, and each of the $443$ contains a triangle.
Thus neither $d=5$ nor $d=6$ is possible.

A structurally different program treats the independent
$4$-sets as a hypergraph and performs triangle-pruned transversal search.
It visits $22{,}335{,}989$ search states over both layers and also finds no
valid neighbourhood.  The Python verifier pins catalogue hashes and record
counts; both implementations check the relevant edge counts and Ramsey
constraints before extension, and the Python verifier also checks order
explicitly.
\end{proof}

\section{The saturated residue at 112 edges is nonempty}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is not a census of the full $112$-edge layer.
To make this distinction constructive, we encoded the saturated residue as
SAT.  There are $\binom{22}{2}=231$ edge variables.  The base formula has
one negative six-literal clause for each $4$-set, one positive ten-literal
clause for each $5$-set, and a sequential counter \cite{sinz-2005} fixing
exactly $112$ edges.  For each potential nonedge $uv$, auxiliary witnesses require that
some adjacent pair $xy$ lies in $N(u)\cap N(v)$; then adding $uv$ creates the
$K_4$ on $\{u,v,x,y\}$.

The deterministic DIMACS instance has $69{,}992$ variables and $356{,}482$
clauses, with hash
\begin{center}
\small\ttfamily
e65db47cc9bc70924b6ea7550cc93d8b8370bffc32b16c4275ca7d5b9d70297f.
\end{center}
Kissat 4.0.4, invoked through PySAT 1.9.dev15
\cite{kissat-2020,pysat-2018}, found a model.  Ordinary nauty and Traces
canonicalize that solver-produced witness; ordinary nauty gives the graph6
record
\begin{verbatim}
U?owqT_T@EelPxeXTiU\goB}QztebfjO\yxgJmq_
\end{verbatim}
It has degree sequence $(11^9,10^9,9^3,8)$, clique number $3$, independence
number $4$, and is $K_4$-saturated.  A direct combinatorial audit and a
separately implemented bit-parallel audit agree on all properties.  The
canonical record, including its terminating newline, has hash
\begin{center}
\small\ttfamily
9f94ad40db08993931c1b798b16e2601afbea7530f2122a0ff6b08a45e017ca4.
\end{center}

\begin{corollary}
$|\Rcal(4,5,22,112)|\geq785{,}889$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Reproducibility and trusted boundary}

The recorded run used macOS on Apple silicon, Python 3.13.1, Apple clang
17.0.0 in C++20 mode, nauty and Traces 2.9300, PySAT 1.9.dev15, and Kissat
4.0.4.  Machine-readable manifests record timings as scale indicators, not
as performance comparisons.

The prepared reproduction release contains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item pinned source-catalogue hashes and the final graph6 catalogue;
  \item primary and separately implemented deletion/filter programs, scripts
  for nauty/Traces canonicalization, and bytewise comparisons;
  \item two structurally different minimum-degree extension implementations;
  \item the deterministic saturated-residue CNF and audited witness;
  \item JSON manifests containing counts, distributions, versions, hashes,
  and validation outcomes; and
  \item unit tests, including exhaustive small-instance tests of the
  cardinality encoder when the pinned optional SAT dependency is installed.
\end{itemize}

The exact class count has an explicit trusted boundary.  We rely on the
published completeness of the input catalogues, our graph6 parsers and
encoders, exhaustive iteration in two deletion/filter implementations, and
canonical labeling by nauty and Traces.  Agreement of the separate coverage
programs and two canonical-labeling algorithms, followed by byte-identical
recanonicalization, is a strong error check but not a formal verification of
those programs.  The final standard-library graph-property audit calls
neither graph-isomorphism library.  Pinned input hashes, generated manifests,
and scripts make every numerical claim here reproducible, except for the
externally established completeness of the source catalogues.

\section{Discussion and the remaining census}

The unknown part of $\Rcal(4,5,22,112)$ consists exactly of its
$K_4$-saturated members, and the witness above proves that it is nonempty.
Two exhaustive continuations are available.

First, choose a maximum-degree root.  The average degree and
$R(3,5)=14$ \cite{radziszowski-survey} force
$11\leq\Delta(G)\leq13$.  The root neighbourhood belongs to
$\Rcal(3,5,d)$ and its nonneighbours induce a graph in
$\Rcal(4,4,21-d)$.  Complete public seed catalogues then give a finite gluing
problem with mixed $K_4$ and independent-$5$ constraints.

Second, the saturated DIMACS formula can be enumerated with SAT Modulo
Symmetries.  A publication-grade coverage claim would retain the labelled
models, independently validate them, verify the symmetry clauses, add a
blocker for each output, and check a final proof-producing UNSAT run.  We do
not report such a census here.

The present calculation is therefore both exact and deliberately limited:
it completes the entire non-$K_4$-saturated sector, proves a degree restriction
for the full layer, and exposes the saturated residue as the sole missing
class.  It does not by itself improve $R(5,5)\leq46$.
